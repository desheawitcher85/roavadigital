If you're a roofing contractor in Roanoke and you're not showing up in the top 3 Google results when someone searches "roofer near me," you're not alone. Most contractors in this market have the same fixable problems. After auditing dozens of local contractor GBP profiles and websites, the same four gaps show up again and again.

Roanoke roofing contractors most often lose local search rankings for four reasons: incomplete Google Business Profile setup, inconsistent business name, address, and phone across directories, no dedicated service area pages on their website, and review velocity that lags behind top-ranked competitors. All four are fixable within 90 days.

## The Google local pack runs on 3 signals

Google ranks local results using three primary factors: **proximity** (how close the business is to the searcher), **relevance** (how well your profile and website match the search), and **prominence** (how authoritative and active your business looks across the web).

Proximity is mostly outside your control. Relevance and prominence are not. That's where most contractors fall short.

## Problem 1: An incomplete Google Business Profile

Walk through the Google Business Profile checklist right now. Most roofing contractors in Roanoke are missing at least 4 to 6 of these:

- Primary business category set to the correct roofing subcategory (not just "Contractor")
- Complete service list with individual services named out
- Service area set at the city and ZIP code level, not just a radius
- Business description using relevant keywords
- Products section populated for major job types
- Weekly posts (photos, storm alerts, job completions)
- Q&A section with at least 5 populated questions and answers
- More than 10 real, recent photos

Google has stated publicly that profile completeness is a ranking factor. Every missing field is a gap your competitors can fill.

## Problem 2: NAP inconsistency across directories

NAP means Name, Address, Phone. Those three data points need to match exactly across every directory where your business is listed: Yelp, Angi, HomeAdvisor, BBB, the Roanoke Chamber, and 40+ others.

Here's what inconsistency looks like in practice:

- Your GBP shows "Smith Roofing LLC" but Yelp shows "Smith Roofing"
- Your address on HomeAdvisor is missing "Suite 100" that GBP has
- An old phone number is still live on 12 directories from 3 years ago

Google uses citation signals to validate that your business is real and accurately represented. Mismatches create doubt. Doubt suppresses rankings.

This is the most unglamorous part of local SEO. It's also one of the most impactful.

## Problem 3: No location-specific website pages

A homepage that says "We serve Roanoke and surrounding areas" in one paragraph won't rank for "roof replacement Vinton VA" or "gutter installation Salem Virginia."

Google matches the search query to the closest page on your site. If you don't have a page specifically about roof replacement in Vinton, you're not competing for that search. Your competitors with dedicated pages are.

The Roanoke MSA has 7 distinct service areas worth targeting:

1. Roanoke City
2. Roanoke County
3. Salem
4. Vinton
5. Botetourt County
6. Bedford County
7. New River Valley

Plus service-type pages: roof replacement, roof repair, gutter installation, gutter replacement, siding installation, emergency roofing. That's a grid of pages worth building over 6 to 12 months.

## Problem 4: Review velocity that lags the market

Check the Google Business Profiles of the top 3 roofing contractors in your primary Roanoke service area right now. Count their reviews. Note the date of their most recent review.

Most top-ranked roofing contractors in Roanoke have 40 to 120+ reviews at 4.6 stars or better. More importantly, they have reviews from the last 2 to 4 weeks, consistently.

A burst of 20 reviews followed by 6 months of silence is worse than 3 reviews per month steadily. Google's algorithm values recency and velocity, not just count.

The fix is simple but requires a system. Most contractors who consistently get reviews use one of these:

- A text sent from the job site immediately after the job completes ("We'd love a review: [link]")
- A follow-up text 48 hours after invoice delivery
- A simple field on the completion paperwork with a QR code

The technology doesn't matter. The consistency does.

## What to fix first

If you're starting from scratch, prioritize in this order:

1. **GBP completeness audit** — 2 to 4 hours of work, immediate impact
2. **NAP citation cleanup** — 1 to 2 months of cleanup work, foundational
3. **Review system** — Set up this week, results visible in 30 to 60 days
4. **Location and service pages** — 3 to 6 month content build

The first three you can start this week. The last one is a sustained content effort.

## FAQ

### How long does it take to rank in the Google local pack in Roanoke?

With consistent work on all four of the issues above, most contractors see measurable local pack movement within 60 to 90 days. Hitting the top 3 for competitive keywords like "roofing company Roanoke VA" typically takes 4 to 6 months of sustained work.

### Is local SEO different for roofing than for other contractors?

Yes in a few specific ways. Roofing has storm-driven search spikes that require seasonal content and bid adjustments. Roofing GBP category selection is more nuanced than general contracting. And the job value justifies a deeper investment in location-specific pages than, say, a lawn care company.

### Can I do local SEO myself or do I need to hire someone?

The GBP completeness audit and review system you can implement yourself with a few hours of work. Citation cleanup is tedious but doable without outside help. Content — location and service pages — typically requires either good writing time or a specialist.

### What does a competitor do that you can't easily replicate?

Reviews are the hardest to replicate fast. If a competitor has 120 reviews and you have 18, getting to parity takes time even with a great review system running. Everything else (GBP, citations, content) is fixable on a shorter timeline.
